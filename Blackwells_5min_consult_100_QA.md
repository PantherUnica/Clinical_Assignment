# Blackwell's Five-Minute Veterinary Consult: Canine and Feline, 6th edition: 100 questions and answers

Each answer is taken from the book's own text for the topic named in brackets. Doses are as printed in the book; check current product data before clinical use.

**1. Why are cats more susceptible to acetaminophen toxicosis than dogs?**  
_[Acetaminophen (APAP) Toxicosis]_  
Cats cannot effectively glucuronidate acetaminophen because of a genetic deficiency in the glucuronide conjugation pathway. They can be poisoned by as little as 50-60 mg/kg (often half a tablet), and rapidly develop methemoglobinemia.

**2. What is the N-acetylcysteine protocol for acetaminophen toxicosis?**  
_[Acetaminophen (APAP) Toxicosis]_  
A loading dose of 140 mg/kg diluted in D5W PO or IV, then 70 mg/kg diluted in D5W PO or IV q6h for 5-7 more treatments. Activated charcoal 2 g/kg PO is given right after emesis or gastric lavage, and SAMe can be added as a glutathione donor.

**3. What is the fomepizole dosing schedule for ethylene glycol toxicosis in dogs?**  
_[Ethylene Glycol Toxicosis]_  
20 mg/kg IV initially, then 15 mg/kg IV at 12 and 24 hours, then 5 mg/kg IV at 36 hours. The book describes fomepizole as an effective, non-toxic liver alcohol dehydrogenase inhibitor that needs less intensive care than ethanol.

**4. Which methylxanthine makes up the largest fraction in chocolate?**  
_[Chocolate (Methylxanthine) Toxicosis]_  
Theobromine. Chocolate toxicosis causes acute gastroenteric, neurologic and cardiac signs from excessive intake of methylxanthine alkaloids.

**5. What are the main effects of xylitol ingestion in dogs, and how fast do signs appear?**  
_[Xylitol Toxicosis]_  
Vomiting, weakness, ataxia, seizures, hypoglycemia and hypokalemia, and in some dogs hepatic failure. Signs may develop within 15-30 minutes, but with sugar-free gums hypoglycemia may be delayed up to 12 hours.

**6. What is the recommended fluid therapy after grape or raisin ingestion in a dog?**  
_[Grape, Raisin, and Currant Toxicosis]_  
Fluid diuresis at 2-3 times maintenance for at least 48 hours, longer if renal failure develops, after GI decontamination with emesis and activated charcoal.

**7. Which species is at risk of systemic lily poisoning, and what organ is affected?**  
_[Lily Toxicosis]_  
Cats. Ingestion of Lilium or Hemerocallis (daylily) leaves or flowers causes a severe nephrotoxic syndrome, with as little as 2-3 leaves reported to be dangerous. Dogs only get mild GI upset.

**8. Why is the coagulopathy from anticoagulant rodenticides delayed?**  
_[Rodenticide Toxicosis—Anticoagulants]_  
Because it results from depletion of the vitamin K1-dependent clotting factors II, VII, IX and X, so onset is delayed by 3-5 days.

**9. How long is vitamin K1 given for first- versus second-generation anticoagulant rodenticides?**  
_[Rodenticide Toxicosis—Anticoagulants]_  
2.5-5.0 mg/kg PO: q12h for 5-7 days for most first-generation products, and q24h for 4-6 weeks for most second-generation products. Feeding a little fat may improve absorption.

**10. Which dogs are most susceptible to ivermectin toxicosis?**  
_[Ivermectin and Other Macrocyclic Lactones Toxicosis]_  
Collies are most commonly affected, along with dogs homozygous for the ABCB1 gene deletion (found in Shetland sheepdogs, Australian shepherds and others). Heterozygous dogs can react at higher doses.

**11. Why is metaldehyde called the 'shake and bake' toxicant?**  
_[Metaldehyde Toxicosis]_  
Because of its characteristic tremors, seizures and hyperthermia. Signs usually develop within 4 hours of ingestion, and there is no specific antidote.

**12. How can cats be exposed to permethrin toxicity, and what treats the tremors?**  
_[Pyrethrin and Pyrethroid Toxicosis]_  
Through direct contact with dogs treated with permethrin spot-ons. Tremors and seizures are treated with methocarbamol 55-220 mg/kg IV, not exceeding 330 mg/kg/day.

**13. What pathologic findings are associated with NSAID toxicosis?**  
_[Nonsteroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drug Toxicosis]_  
GI irritation, ulceration or hemorrhage with possible gastric perforation and peritonitis, plus renal tubular or papillary necrosis or interstitial nephritis. H2 blockers such as ranitidine or famotidine are used for GI ulceration.

**14. How long can signs be delayed after a pit viper bite, and is local tissue reaction a reliable guide?**  
_[Snake Venom Toxicosis—Pit Vipers]_  
Signs may be delayed for up to 8 hours after envenomation, and tissue reaction around the bite is not a reliable indicator of systemic envenomation.

**15. Which breeds are at increased risk of hypoadrenocorticism?**  
_[Hypoadrenocorticism (Addison's Disease)]_  
Great Danes, rottweilers, Portuguese water dogs, standard poodles, bearded collies, Leonbergers, West Highland white terriers, Nova Scotia duck tolling retrievers and Wheaten terriers. Golden retrievers and Chihuahuas have a decreased risk.

**16. What is the long-term treatment for chronic primary hypoadrenocorticism?**  
_[Hypoadrenocorticism (Addison's Disease)]_  
Daily glucocorticoid replacement (prednisone 0.1-0.2 mg/kg/day) plus mineralocorticoid replacement with DOCP (Percorten) 2.2 mg/kg IM or SC, usually monthly, adjusted on serial electrolytes.

**17. What is the difference between spontaneous and iatrogenic hyperadrenocorticism?**  
_[Hyperadrenocorticism (Cushing's Syndrome)—Dogs]_  
Spontaneous HAC is excessive cortisol production by the adrenal cortex. Iatrogenic HAC results from excessive exogenous glucocorticoids given in any form or by any route.

**18. How does mitotane work in canine hyperadrenocorticism?**  
_[Hyperadrenocorticism (Cushing's Syndrome)—Dogs]_  
Mitotane (o,p'-DDD, Lysodren) selectively destroys the glucocorticoid-secreting cells of the adrenal cortex. It is one of two main drugs for pituitary-dependent HAC and may be the drug of choice for adrenal tumors because it may destroy tumor cells too.

**19. What is the initial insulin protocol for diabetic ketoacidosis?**  
_[Diabetes Mellitus with Ketoacidosis]_  
Regular insulin is the insulin of choice, starting at 0.2 U/kg IM (or SC if hydration is normal), then 0.1-0.2 U/kg 3-6 hours later, which may be given hourly if needed.

**20. What is the starting dose of porcine lente insulin (Vetsulin) in cats?**  
_[Diabetes Mellitus without Complication—Cats]_  
2 U per cat SC q12h. Protamine zinc insulin (PZI, U-40) is a long-acting alternative usually given SC q24h.

**21. What is the starting dose of Vetsulin in diabetic dogs, and what syringe must be used?**  
_[Diabetes Mellitus without Complication—Dogs]_  
0.75 units/kg SC q12h. It is a U-40 insulin, so it must be used with a U-40 insulin syringe.

**22. What is the oral methimazole dose for feline hyperthyroidism, and what is the most effective treatment?**  
_[Hyperthyroidism]_  
Methimazole 5-15 mg/day divided q8-12h; it can also be given transdermally in PLO gel, though that is slower. Radioiodine therapy is described as arguably the safest and most effective treatment.

**23. Which dog breeds are predisposed to hypothyroidism?**  
_[Hypothyroidism]_  
Larger breeds such as golden retriever, Doberman pinscher, Great Dane and Irish setter, and some smaller breeds including miniature schnauzer, cocker spaniel, poodle and dachshund. It is most common in middle-aged dogs.

**24. What is the long-term prednisone dose for insulinoma if diet fails?**  
_[Insulinoma]_  
Start at 0.25 mg/kg PO q12h and increase as needed to 2-3 mg/kg PO q12h. Diazoxide is another option.

**25. How is hypertension controlled before and during pheochromocytoma surgery?**  
_[Pheochromocytoma]_  
With phentolamine 0.02-0.1 mg/kg IV to effect. Boxers, miniature poodles and German shepherds are predisposed, with a median age of 11 years.

**26. How is central diabetes insipidus treated?**  
_[Diabetes Insipidus]_  
With DDAVP: 1-2 drops of the intranasal preparation in the conjunctival sac q12-24h, or 2-5 µg SC q12-24h. An oral DDAVP tablet is also available.

**27. What blood pressure values define systemic hypertension in dogs and cats?**  
_[Hypertension, Systemic]_  
Systolic above 160 mmHg or diastolic above 120 mmHg, measured by any method.

**28. What is the first-line drug for systemic hypertension in cats?**  
_[Hypertension, Systemic]_  
Amlodipine, with an ACE inhibitor added if proteinuria (UPC above 0.4) is present. In dogs an ACE inhibitor is used.

**29. Which breeds are at increased risk of severe parvovirus enteritis?**  
_[Canine Parvovirus Infection]_  
Rottweiler, Doberman pinscher, American pit bull terrier, Labrador retriever, German shepherd and Yorkshire terrier.

**30. What causes canine distemper, and is there an effective antiviral?**  
_[Canine Distemper]_  
Canine distemper virus, a Morbillivirus in the Paramyxoviridae family. No antiviral drugs are known to be effective, so treatment is supportive, with broad-spectrum antibiotics for secondary infection because CDV is highly immunosuppressive.

**31. What virus causes feline panleukopenia and what cells does it target?**  
_[Feline Panleukopenia]_  
Feline parvovirus, which infects only mitotic (rapidly dividing) cells and causes their acute lysis. Clinically it causes sudden depression, vomiting, diarrhea, severe dehydration and high mortality.

**32. What are the hallmark features of FIP?**  
_[Feline Infectious Peritonitis (FIP)]_  
Insidious onset, persistent fever that does not respond to treatment, pyogranulomatous tissue reaction, exudative effusions in body cavities and high mortality.

**33. Which FeLV subgroup is most transmissible?**  
_[Feline Leukemia Virus Infection (FeLV)]_  
FeLV-A. It is present in all isolates. The other subgroups are B, C and T.

**34. What is the dose of zidovudine for FIV and what must be monitored?**  
_[Feline Immunodeficiency Virus Infection (FIV)]_  
5-15 mg/kg PO q12h. It is most effective against acute infection, and bone marrow toxicity must be monitored.

**35. What is the typical signalment of dogs with heartworm disease?**  
_[Heartworm Disease—Dogs]_  
Medium to large breeds more than small dogs, outdoor more than indoor dogs, most aged 3-8 years, and males more often than females.

**36. What antibiotic is used to clear Leptospira from the kidneys?**  
_[Leptospirosis]_  
Dihydrostreptomycin 10-15 mg/kg IM q12h for 2 weeks. Procaine penicillin G is used first until kidney function returns to normal.

**37. What are the antibiotic options for Lyme disease in dogs?**  
_[Lyme Borreliosis]_  
Doxycycline 5-10 mg/kg PO q12h with food, amoxicillin 20 mg/kg PO q8-12h, or azithromycin 25 mg/kg PO q24h. Doxycycline is preferred if Anaplasma phagocytophilum co-infection is present.

**38. What is the treatment of choice for canine ehrlichiosis?**  
_[Ehrlichiosis (Anaplasma)]_  
Doxycycline 5 mg/kg PO q12h for 4 weeks, given IV for 5 days if the dog is vomiting.

**39. What drug is FDA-approved for babesiosis and at what dose?**  
_[Babesiosis]_  
Imidocarb dipropionate 6.6 mg/kg SC or IM every 1-2 weeks. It may clear B. canis but not B. gibsoni.

**40. What is the fenbendazole regimen for giardiasis in dogs and cats?**  
_[Giardiasis]_  
50 mg/kg PO q24h for 3 days in dogs or 5 days in cats; a second course may be needed. It should be combined with environmental cleaning and bathing the patient.

**41. What is the recommended treatment once rabies is diagnosed?**  
_[Rabies]_  
There is no treatment. Once the diagnosis is certain, euthanasia is indicated, and suspected cases must be securely isolated.

**42. What is the initial antibiotic choice for uncomplicated canine infectious respiratory disease?**  
_[Canine Infectious Respiratory Disease]_  
Amoxicillin/clavulanic acid 12.5-25 mg/kg PO q12h or doxycycline 5 mg/kg PO q12h.

**43. What signs characterize feline herpesvirus infection?**  
_[Feline Herpesvirus Infection]_  
Sneezing, fever, rhinitis, conjunctivitis and ulcerative keratitis. Kittens are most susceptible, and brachycephalic breeds get more severe corneal disease and corneal sequestra.

**44. What clinical forms can feline calicivirus cause?**  
_[Feline Calicivirus Infection]_  
Upper respiratory disease, oral ulceration, pneumonia, occasionally arthritis, or a highly fatal systemic hemorrhagic disease.

**45. What is the clindamycin dose for toxoplasmosis?**  
_[Toxoplasmosis]_  
25-50 mg/kg PO or IM daily divided into two doses, continued for at least 2 weeks after clinical signs clear. Topical 1% prednisone drops q8h are used for uveitis.

**46. Which dog breeds are predisposed to pancreatitis?**  
_[Pancreatitis—Dogs]_  
Miniature schnauzer, Yorkshire terrier and cocker spaniel. Acute pancreatitis is most common in middle-aged and older dogs, with a mean age of 6.5 years.

**47. What are the most common signs of pancreatitis in cats?**  
_[Pancreatitis—Cats]_  
Vague, nonspecific signs, most often anorexia, lethargy and vomiting. Siamese cats are predisposed, and the mean age for acute pancreatitis is 7.3 years.

**48. Which breeds are predisposed to exocrine pancreatic insufficiency?**  
_[Exocrine Pancreatic Insufficiency]_  
German shepherd dogs, rough-coated collies and Eurasians. In young adult dogs it is usually caused by pancreatic acinar atrophy.

**49. How are pancreatic enzymes dosed for EPI?**  
_[Exocrine Pancreatic Insufficiency]_  
Powdered enzymes mixed into food at 1 teaspoon per 10 kg body weight with each meal, with at least two meals a day. Pre-incubating the enzymes with food does not improve their effect.

**50. What is GDV and which dogs are at risk?**  
_[Gastric Dilation and Volvulus Syndrome]_  
A condition where the stomach dilates and then rotates around its short axis. Large, deep-chested breeds such as Great Danes and German shepherds are at risk, and risk increases with age.

**51. What is megaesophagus and which cats are predisposed?**  
_[Megaesophagus]_  
A focal or generalized dilation of the esophagus with reduced or absent peristalsis. Siamese and Siamese-related cats are predisposed.

**52. Which dogs are most commonly affected by hemorrhagic gastroenteritis?**  
_[Gastroenteritis, Hemorrhagic]_  
Small and toy breeds, especially miniature schnauzer, dachshund, Yorkshire terrier and miniature poodle. It usually affects adults with a mean age of 5 years.

**53. How is IBD categorized?**  
_[Inflammatory Bowel Disease]_  
By the predominant mucosal cellular infiltrate, for example lymphocytic-plasmacytic. It is a group of chronic enteropathies with persistent or intermittent GI signs and histologic intestinal inflammation.

**54. What cytology finding supports hepatic lipidosis in cats?**  
_[Hepatic Lipidosis]_  
On fine-needle liver aspiration, more than 80% of hepatocytes show severe cytosolic vacuolation. Vitamin K1 is recommended for all cats with suspected lipidosis.

**55. Where are congenital portosystemic shunts usually located in small dogs and cats?**  
_[Portosystemic Vascular Anomaly, Congenital]_  
Extrahepatic shunts are more common in small-breed dogs and cats. Small purebred and mixed 'terrier' dogs are at higher risk.

**56. How long must kidney disease be present to be called chronic?**  
_[Renal Failure, Chronic]_  
More than 3 months. Mean age at diagnosis is about 7 years in dogs and 9 years in cats.

**57. How is prerenal azotemia distinguished from acute kidney injury?**  
_[Renal Failure, Acute]_  
Prerenal azotemia has concentrated urine (USG at least 1.030 in dogs and 1.035 in cats) and is correctable with fluid repletion.

**58. Can calcium oxalate uroliths be dissolved medically?**  
_[Urolithiasis, Calcium Oxalate]_  
No. No available drugs effectively dissolve them, so they are removed. Steroids and furosemide should be used with care because they promote calciuria.

**59. Which six dog breeds make up 60% of calcium oxalate urolith cases?**  
_[Urolithiasis, Calcium Oxalate]_  
Miniature schnauzer, Lhasa apso, Yorkshire terrier, bichon frise, shih tzu and miniature poodle.

**60. What are most uroliths in dogs under 12 months old?**  
_[Urolithiasis, Struvite—Dogs]_  
Infection-induced struvite (magnesium ammonium phosphate) uroliths.

**61. Which breeds are predisposed to urate uroliths?**  
_[Urolithiasis, Urate]_  
Dalmatians, English bulldogs, and breeds at risk of portosystemic shunts such as Yorkshire terriers.

**62. When after estrus do dogs and cats with pyometra usually present?**  
_[Pyometra]_  
Dogs within 12 weeks after their last estrus, and cats within 4 weeks of their last estrus. It is life-threatening when the cervix is closed and the bitch is systemically ill.

**63. When does eclampsia usually occur and which breeds are at risk?**  
_[Eclampsia]_  
Usually 1-4 weeks postpartum, most often before day 40. It is most common in toy breeds such as Chihuahua, miniature pinscher, Shih Tzu, miniature poodle and Pomeranian, and in first litters.

**64. How is eclampsia treated in the bitch?**  
_[Eclampsia]_  
10% calcium gluconate 0.22-0.44 mL/kg IV slowly over 5 minutes to effect, monitoring heart rate or ECG. Hyperthermia and hypoglycemia are corrected, and she is sent home on oral calcium 10-30 mg/kg PO q8h.

**65. Why are brachycephalic breeds prone to dystocia?**  
_[Dystocia]_  
Because they combine a broad fetal head with a narrow maternal pelvis, as in the Bulldog, Boston terrier and pug.

**66. Which breeds are at high risk of lymphoma?**  
_[Lymphoma—Dogs]_  
Boxer, basset hound, golden retriever, Saint Bernard, Scottish terrier, Airedale terrier and bulldog. Dachshunds and Pomeranians are low-risk.

**67. What is the typical age of cats with lymphoma by FeLV status?**  
_[Lymphoma—Cats]_  
About 3 years in FeLV-positive cats and 7 years in FeLV-negative cats. Lymphoma is also the most common spinal cord tumor in cats.

**68. What is the standard chemotherapy after amputation for canine osteosarcoma?**  
_[Osteosarcoma]_  
A platinum drug (cisplatin or carboplatin) or doxorubicin, with at least four doses recommended.

**69. What proportion of canine splenic tumors are hemangiosarcomas, and which breeds are predisposed?**  
_[Hemangiosarcoma, Spleen and Liver]_  
Up to 70%. German shepherds, Labrador retrievers and golden retrievers are predisposed.

**70. What is the vinblastine and prednisone protocol for canine mast cell tumors?**  
_[Mast Cell Tumors]_  
Vinblastine 2.0 mg/m2 IV every 7 days for 4 doses, then every 14 days for 4 doses, with prednisone 1 mg/kg PO q24h.

**71. What proportion of feline mammary tumors are malignant?**  
_[Mammary Gland Tumors—Cats]_  
85-95%, mostly carcinomas. Siamese cats have twice the risk of other breeds.

**72. What is the mean age of dogs with malignant versus benign mammary tumors?**  
_[Mammary Gland Tumors—Dogs]_  
9-11 years for malignant and 7-9 years for benign tumors. They are uncommon in dogs under 5 years.

**73. What is the vincristine protocol for transmissible venereal tumor?**  
_[Transmissible Venereal Tumor]_  
0.5-0.7 mg/m2 IV once weekly, continued for 2 weeks beyond complete resolution of visible disease. Doxorubicin can be tried if remission is partial.

**74. Which breeds are predisposed to dilated cardiomyopathy?**  
_[Cardiomyopathy, Dilated—Dogs]_  
Doberman pinscher and boxer, giant breeds (Scottish deerhound, Irish wolfhound, Great Dane, Saint Bernard, Afghan hound, Newfoundland), cocker spaniel and Portuguese water dog.

**75. Which cat breeds are predisposed to hypertrophic cardiomyopathy?**  
_[Cardiomyopathy, Hypertrophic—Cats]_  
Maine coon, ragdoll, Sphynx, British and American shorthairs, and Persians. Typical age is 5-7 years.

**76. What furosemide dose is used for a critically dyspneic cat with HCM?**  
_[Cardiomyopathy, Hypertrophic—Cats]_  
4 mg/kg IV, which can be repeated in 1 hour if the cat is still severely dyspneic. The usual dose is 1-2 mg/kg q8-24h.

**77. Which dogs typically develop myxomatous mitral valve disease?**  
_[Atrioventricular Valve (Myxomatous) Disease]_  
Small breeds under 20 kg, such as Cavalier King Charles spaniels, Chihuahuas, miniature schnauzers, Maltese, Pomeranians, cocker spaniels, Pekingese and poodles.

**78. What is a patent ductus arteriosus and when is it usually found?**  
_[Patent Ductus Arteriosus]_  
Persistent patency of the fetal ductus connecting the descending aorta to the pulmonary artery. Most are found during the initial vaccination visits, and females are predisposed in many breeds.

**79. What is the main feature of boxer cardiomyopathy?**  
_[Cardiomyopathy—Boxer (Arrhythmogenic Right Ventricular)]_  
Ventricular tachyarrhythmias, which may cause syncope or sudden death. Fewer than 5% of dogs develop congestive heart failure with systolic dysfunction.

**80. When should antiepileptic treatment be started in a dog with genetic epilepsy?**  
_[Epilepsy, Genetic (Idiopathic)—Dogs]_  
At the second generalized seizure if the dog is under 2 years old, and in other dogs when the interval between seizures is gradually getting shorter.

**81. Which breeds get Hansen type I disc disease?**  
_[Intervertebral Disc Disease, Thoracolumbar]_  
Dachshund, shih tzu, Lhasa apso, Pekingese, cocker spaniel, Welsh corgi, and toy and miniature poodles. Type II disease affects mainly large breeds.

**82. Which breed accounts for about half of wobbler syndrome cases?**  
_[Cervical Spondylomyelopathy (Wobbler Syndrome)]_  
The Doberman pinscher. Great Dane, rottweiler, Weimaraner and Dalmatian also have a high incidence.

**83. What gene mutation causes degenerative myelopathy, and is there an effective drug?**  
_[Degenerative Myelopathy]_  
A mutation in the superoxide dismutase 1 gene (SOD1). No drug has been proven to slow or halt progression.

**84. What is the pyridostigmine dose for myasthenia gravis?**  
_[Myasthenia Gravis]_  
1-3 mg/kg PO q8-12h, with the syrup diluted half and half in water.

**85. What is geriatric vestibular disease in dogs?**  
_[Vestibular Disease, Geriatric—Dogs]_  
An acute, non-progressive disturbance of the peripheral vestibular system in older dogs, usually over 8 years old. The cause is unknown.

**86. Which breeds have an increased incidence of cranial cruciate ligament disease at a young age?**  
_[Cruciate Ligament Disease, Cranial]_  
Rottweilers and Labrador retrievers under 4 years old. The West Highland white terrier is the overrepresented small breed.

**87. Which dogs are most commonly affected by patellar luxation?**  
_[Patellar Luxation]_  
Toy and miniature breeds, such as miniature and toy poodles, Yorkshire terriers, Pomeranians, Pekingese, Chihuahuas and Boston terriers.

**88. What is panosteitis and at what age does it usually occur?**  
_[Panosteitis]_  
A self-limiting, painful condition of the long bones of young medium to large-breed dogs, seen radiographically as increased marrow density. It usually occurs at 5-18 months of age, mostly in males.

**89. Which breeds are predisposed to Demodex canis demodicosis?**  
_[Demodicosis]_  
American Staffordshire terrier, Shar-Pei, Boston terrier, English bulldog and West Highland white terrier.

**90. Which cats and dogs are predisposed to dermatophytosis?**  
_[Dermatophytosis]_  
Longhaired cats such as Persians and Himalayans, and Yorkshire and Manchester terriers. M. canis is more common in younger animals.

**91. How is selamectin used for scabies in dogs?**  
_[Sarcoptic Mange]_  
It is labeled every 30 days, but application every 2 weeks for at least three treatments may be more effective.

**92. What intraocular pressure is diagnostic of glaucoma in dogs and cats?**  
_[Glaucoma]_  
Above 25-30 mmHg in dogs and above 31 mmHg in cats.

**93. What Schirmer tear test values indicate KCS in dogs?**  
_[Keratoconjunctivitis Sicca]_  
Normal is at least 15 mm/min of wetting; symptomatic dogs usually have less than 10 mm/min. Treatment uses lacrimostimulants such as cyclosporine 0.2% ointment or tacrolimus q12h.

**94. Why should the prolapsed third eyelid gland be replaced rather than removed?**  
_[Prolapsed Gland of the Third Eyelid (Cherry Eye)]_  
Because the gland produces up to 50% of the aqueous tear film, and excising it predisposes to KCS. Surgical replacement (imbrication technique) is recommended.

**95. What cough suppressants are used for tracheal collapse?**  
_[Tracheal and Airway Collapse]_  
Butorphanol 0.5-1 mg/kg PO q4-8h or hydrocodone 0.22 mg/kg PO q4-8h, reduced to the least frequent dosing that controls the cough.

**96. What components make up brachycephalic airway syndrome?**  
_[Brachycephalic Airway Syndrome]_  
Stenotic nares, overlong soft palate, everted laryngeal saccules and laryngeal collapse; a hypoplastic trachea may also be present. English bulldogs are most commonly affected (up to 55% of the breed).

**97. What is the emergency treatment for a cat in an asthma crisis?**  
_[Asthma, Bronchitis—Cats]_  
Oxygen and a parenteral bronchodilator such as terbutaline 0.01 mg/kg IV or SC, repeated if there is no improvement in 20-30 minutes.

**98. What is the starting corticosteroid dose for IMHA and when is it reduced?**  
_[Anemia, Immune-Mediated]_  
Prednisone 1-2 mg/kg/day divided q12h for 2-4 weeks (prednisolone in cats). Once the PCV is above 30%, the dose drops to 1 mg/kg q12h and is then tapered by no more than 25-50% per month over 3-6 months.

**99. How is desmopressin used before surgery in dogs with von Willebrand disease?**  
_[Von Willebrand Disease]_  
1 µg/kg SC given 30 minutes before surgery in dogs with mild to moderate Type 1 vWD. The response is variable.

**100. Which animals are predisposed to heat stroke?**  
_[Heat Stroke and Hyperthermia]_  
Longhaired and brachycephalic animals, very young or old animals, young dogs that overexert, and old dogs with preexisting disease. Cats are uncommonly affected.
