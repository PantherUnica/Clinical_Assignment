**2. When should acepromazine be avoided?**
_[Acepromazine]_

**Book answer:** In hypotension due to shock, trauma or cardiovascular disease, in animals under 3 months old and in animals with liver disease. Large breeds are very sensitive when dosed on a mg/kg basis, so use the low end of the range. In Boxers, use low doses (0.01 mg/kg i.m.) or avoid it, because fainting and syncope from sinoatrial block can occur.

**AI answer:**

Acepromazine should be avoided or not used in the following situations:

- Hypotension due to shock, trauma, or cardiovascular disease, where acepromazine is contraindicated. [1]
- Animals under 3 months of age. [1]
- Animals with liver disease. [1]
- Boxers, where spontaneous fainting and syncope can occur due to sinoatrial block caused by excessive vagal tone; use low doses (0.01 mg/kg i.m.) or avoid ACP in this breed. [1]
- Boxers and giant breeds at doses >0.025 mg/kg. [2]
- Management of sound phobias in dogs, such as firework or thunder phobia, as ACP is not recommended by behaviourists for this use. [1]
- Fear and aggression in veterinary visits when used as the sole agent, because acepromazine does not provide anxiolysis. [3]
- In combination with oral clonidine, due to potential for serious fluctuations in blood pressure. [3]

The provided evidence does not cover all possible contraindications, precautions, species-specific considerations, or drug interactions for acepromazine.
